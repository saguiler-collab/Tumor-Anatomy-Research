Figure 1

The same three components, one arrow reversed

Current practice runs the top row: a method produces cell fractions, the anatomic pattern falls out, and it is reported as a finding — so the method cannot fail. Writing the ordering down first turns the same pattern into a prediction the method can miss. Nothing is testable while the anatomy is an output; everything is, once it is an input.

What was predicted, before anything was run

Five structures, periphery to core. Each panel shows the ordering a pathologist would predict for one cell type — height is rank, not value. Figure 4

The experiment: does anatomy agree with truth?

A leaderboard on its own proves nothing — a method could satisfy every constraint and still be badly calibrated. The test is whether the ACS ranking agrees with the ranking from real ground truth: — pseudobulk mixtures pooled from — held-out donors whose cells never entered the reference. The bar was fixed before this was computed — rho ≥ 0.60 with a bootstrap CI excluding zero — and both conditions hold. Points on the diagonal are methods the anatomy and the truth rank identically.

Figure 7

Why prognosis is not answerable here

The smallest C-index difference a cohort can distinguish from zero falls as 1.96·√2/√n. Ivy GAP publishes no vital-status column, so even reading every recorded survival time as a death — the most generous reading available, and one this pipeline will only make when asked by name — the cohort reaches — events. That detects a difference of —, which is wider than the interpretable range of the statistic. This is a finished result, not an outstanding task: no method ranking on prognosis from this dataset could mean anything.
